CHAPTER 1
Chapter 1

**Chapter 1 – Scene 1**

The wind howled like a disgruntled spirit as Feyre trudged through the ankle-deep snow, her fleece-lined boots sinking with every step. The forest was a graveyard of skeletal trees, their branches clawing at the bruised twilight sky. Her breath came in ragged puffs, fogging the air before her, and the cold gnawed at her cheeks despite the thick scarf wound around her neck—a borrowed treasure from Mor’s closet, the red-and-gray wool still smelling faintly of her sister’s vanilla perfume.

She adjusted the strap of her bow, the weight of it familiar against her shoulder. *Hunting. Again.* Not that she minded the work—it kept her hands busy, her mind sharp—but the relentless winter had turned the woods into a frozen wasteland. The game had either fled or burrowed deep, leaving her with nothing but empty traps and a growling stomach.

A twig snapped behind her.

Feyre froze, fingers twitching toward the dagger at her hip. The forest held its breath. Then—

*"You’re going to freeze solid before you bag a single rabbit."*

The voice was smooth, amused, and entirely unwelcome.

She didn’t turn. "Go away, Rhysand."

A chuckle, low and warm, curled around her. *"And miss the chance to watch you glare at me? Not a chance."*

Footsteps crunched in the snow, and then he was there—leaning against a tree like he had all the time in the world, his dark coat blending into the shadows. His silver-streaked hair caught the last of the dying light, and his smirk was as infuriating as ever.

Feyre exhaled through her nose. "I don’t need a babysitter."

*"No?"* He pushed off the tree, sauntering closer. *"Then why is your stomach growling loud enough to wake the dead?"*

She shot him a look. "I ate breakfast."

*"Breakfast was six hours ago."* His violet eyes gleamed. *"And it was a single piece of bread."*

She scowled. "How do you even—"

*"I have my ways."* He grinned, all sharp teeth. *"Come on. The Night Court’s kitchens are stocked, and I’m not letting you waste away out here."*

Feyre hesitated. Pride warred with hunger. Hunger won.

"Fine," she muttered. "But I’m not eating anything weird."

Rhysand’s grin widened. *"No promises."*

---

The transition was instant—a blink, and the frozen forest dissolved into the warm, golden glow of the Night Court’s dining hall. Feyre stumbled slightly, her boots meeting polished marble instead of snow. The scent of roasted meat and spiced wine wrapped around her, and her stomach betrayed her with an embarrassingly loud growl.

Rhysand laughed, steering her toward the table. "Sit. Before you faint."

She dropped into a chair, eyeing the spread with suspicion. "You just *happened* to have a feast ready?"

*"I had a feeling."* He poured her a glass of wine, the liquid catching the candlelight like liquid rubies. *"And I know better than to let you starve yourself."*

Feyre took the glass, sipping cautiously. The wine was rich, sweet, with a hint of something darker beneath. "I don’t starve myself."

*"You forget I’ve seen your pantry."*

She choked on her drink.

Rhysand patted her back, far too amused. *"Relax. I won’t tell anyone you’re secretly a squirrel hoarding nuts for winter."*

Feyre glared at him over the rim of her glass. "You’re insufferable."

*"And yet, here you are, eating my food."*

She stabbed a piece of roasted venison with more force than necessary. "Only because I’m too tired to argue."

*"Mmm."* He leaned back, watching her with that infuriating half-smile. *"Liar."*

Feyre ignored him, shoving the meat into her mouth. It was tender, juicy, the flavors exploding across her tongue. She swallowed, then pointed her fork at him. "If you tell Mor I ate like a starving wolf, I will *end* you."

Rhysand pressed a hand to his chest. *"My lips are sealed."*

*"They’d better be."*

A comfortable silence settled between them as Feyre ate, the warmth of the hall seeping into her bones. For the first time in days, her shoulders relaxed.

Rhysand swirled his wine, studying her. *"You know, if you’re this determined to freeze to death, I could always—"*

*"Don’t even say it."*

*"—offer you a warmer climate."*

Feyre set her fork down. "I’m not leaving the Spring Court."

*"Not even for a little vacation?"* He wiggled his fingers, and shadows danced at his fingertips. *"A few weeks in the Night Court. No snow. No hunting. Just… relaxation."*

She narrowed her eyes. "What’s the catch?"

*"No catch."* His voice was too smooth. *"Just a friend offering a favor."*

Feyre exhaled, rubbing her temples. "Rhys—"

*"Think about it."* He stood, the movement fluid, effortless. *"I’ll be in the library if you change your mind."*

She watched him go, the door clicking shut behind him. The fire crackled in the hearth, casting flickering light across the empty plates.

A vacation. No snow. No hunting.

No responsibilities.

Feyre picked up her wine, swirling it thoughtfully.

Then she set it down.

*No. I can’t.*

But as she pushed back from the table, her stomach full and the weight of winter pressing down on her again, she couldn’t shake the image of Rhysand’s smirk—or the way his offer had sounded an awful lot like temptation.
