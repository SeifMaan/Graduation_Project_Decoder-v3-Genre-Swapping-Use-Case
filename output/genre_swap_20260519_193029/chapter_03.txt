**Chapter 3**
**Scene 1**

The wind howled like a scorned lover as we stepped into the snow, its icy fingers clawing at the edges of my hood. The world beyond the cabin door was a monochrome painting—endless white, broken only by the skeletal arms of bare trees and the smudge of distant mountains. My breath curled in front of me, a fleeting ghost before the cold swallowed it whole.

Beside me, Rhysand adjusted the strap of the pack slung over his shoulder, his breath steady, unhurried. The snow crunched beneath his boots, each step deliberate, as if he were walking through a ballroom rather than a frozen wasteland. He shot me a sideways glance, his lips quirking. "You’re glaring at the snow like it personally insulted you."

I huffed, my exhale turning to frost. "It *did* personally insult me. It’s cold, it’s wet, and it’s trying to kill me."

Rhys laughed, the sound rich and warm, a stark contrast to the biting air. "Dramatic. It’s just snow. You’ve survived worse."

I elbowed him lightly, but my gloved fingers barely made an impression through the layers of his coat. "Easy for you to say. You’re not the one who’s going to be a human icicle by nightfall."

He caught my hand before I could pull away, his fingers curling around mine, warm even through the wool. "I’d never let that happen," he murmured, his voice dropping to that low, intimate pitch that made my stomach flip. His thumb brushed over the silver-and-sapphire band on my left hand, a silent reminder of promises made. "Besides, I have it on good authority that you’re *very* hard to kill."

I rolled my eyes, but the corners of my mouth betrayed me, tugging upward. "Flattery won’t get you out of carrying me when my toes fall off from frostbite."

Rhys grinned, unrepentant. "I’ll carry you regardless. Though I *do* prefer you with all your toes intact."

A laugh bubbled out of me, bright and unexpected, cutting through the oppressive silence of the winter landscape. The sound seemed to startle a flock of birds from a nearby tree, their wings beating against the sky as they took flight. For a moment, the world felt less bleak.

Then the wind shifted, and the scent of pine and woodsmoke curled around us, sharp and familiar. My steps faltered. "We’re close."

Rhys nodded, his expression softening. "The border isn’t far. Another mile, maybe two."

I swallowed hard, my fingers tightening around his. The last time I’d crossed into Prythian, I’d been running for my life. Now, I was walking back willingly—no, *choosing* to walk back. The weight of that decision settled over me, heavy as the snow-laden branches above.

Rhys must have sensed the shift in my mood, because he slowed, turning to face me fully. His free hand lifted, brushing a stray lock of hair from my face, tucking it behind my ear. The gesture was so tender it made my chest ache. "You don’t have to do this," he said quietly. "Not if you’re not ready."

I met his gaze, those endless violet eyes filled with a warmth that steadied me. "I *am* ready," I said, and I meant it. Mostly. "I just… didn’t expect it to feel like this."

"Like what?"

"Like stepping back into a dream I’d convinced myself was a nightmare." I exhaled, watching the cloud of breath dissipate between us. "Like I’m finally admitting I missed it."

Rhys’s thumb traced the line of my jaw, his touch featherlight. "You don’t have to miss it anymore."

I leaned into his palm, closing my eyes for the briefest moment. When I opened them again, the world was sharper, clearer. The snow no longer felt like an enemy. The wind no longer felt like a warning.

I straightened, squaring my shoulders. "Come on, High Lord. Let’s go home."

Rhys’s grin returned, sharp and wicked. "Home, huh?"

I shoved him playfully. "Don’t get used to it."

He caught my wrist before I could pull away, tugging me closer until our bodies were nearly flush. His voice dropped to a whisper, his lips brushing the shell of my ear. "Too late."

A shiver ran down my spine—one that had nothing to do with the cold.

Then, without warning, he released me, stepping back with a smirk. "Race you to the border?"

I blinked. "You’re *insufferable*."

"And yet, you love me."

I opened my mouth to retort, but he was already moving, his long strides eating up the distance as he sprinted through the snow. I cursed under my breath, my competitive streak flaring to life, and took off after him.

The wind roared in my ears as I ran, the cold air stinging my lungs. Snow kicked up around my boots, the world blurring into a rush of white and gray. Rhys was ahead of me, his laughter carried back on the wind, taunting me.

I gritted my teeth and pushed harder.

By the time I caught up to him, we were both breathless, our cheeks flushed from the cold and the exertion. Rhys was leaning against the trunk of a towering oak, his arms crossed, looking far too pleased with himself.

I doubled over, hands on my knees, gasping. "You… cheated."

"Did not," he said, grinning. "You’re just slow."

I shot him a glare, but there was no heat behind it. "Next time, I’m leaving you behind."

Rhys pushed off the tree, closing the distance between us in two strides. His hands found my waist, pulling me against him. "Liar," he murmured, his voice rough with amusement. "You’d miss me too much."

I huffed, but my traitorous body leaned into his touch. "Maybe."

His lips brushed mine, soft and fleeting, before he pulled back just enough to meet my eyes. "Admit it."

I rolled my eyes, but the smile tugging at my lips gave me away. "Fine. I’d miss you."

Rhys’s grin was triumphant. "Knew it."

I shoved him again, but this time, he didn’t let go. Instead, he turned me in his arms, so my back was pressed to his chest, and pointed toward the horizon.

There, just visible through the trees, was the shimmering veil of the border—an iridescent curtain of magic that marked the edge of Prythian. Beyond it, the world seemed to breathe, the air warmer, the colors richer. The scent of spring—fresh grass, blooming flowers—drifted toward us, carried on a breeze that didn’t bite.

Home.

Rhys’s arms tightened around me, his chin resting on my shoulder. "Ready?"

I took a deep breath, the familiar magic of the border prickling against my skin. "Yeah," I said softly. "I am."

And then, together, we stepped forward.
