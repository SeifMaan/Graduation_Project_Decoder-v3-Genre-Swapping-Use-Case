CHAPTER 5
A Light in the Dark

**The Morning After**

The bed was too soft.

That was the first thought to claw its way through the fog of sleep, dragging me back to consciousness. The second was that my mouth tasted like a tavern floor after a particularly rowdy night—bitter, stale, and vaguely metallic. The third, and most alarming, was the weight of something cold and heavy on my left hand.

I pried one eye open. Sunlight—*actual sunlight*—streamed through the windows, painting the room in gold and making my head throb in protest. The silver-and-sapphire band on my finger glinted, catching the light like a mocking wink.

*Oh, right.*

I groaned, flopping onto my back. The events of last night rushed back in fragments: the revel, the wine, the way Rhysand had looked at me when he’d asked if I was sure. The way I’d said *yes* before I could second-guess myself. The way my heart had pounded as he slid that ring onto my finger, his fingers warm against mine, his voice low and rough with something that wasn’t quite laughter.

And then—

I bolted upright, the room spinning. The *other* revelations. The threats. The things we’d learned. The weight of the Night Court’s future pressing down on my shoulders like a physical force.

A knock at the door.

I groaned again, pulling the blankets over my head. “Go away.”

The door creaked open anyway. “That’s no way to greet your High Lord,” Rhysand drawled, amusement threading through his voice. “Especially not after last night.”

I peeked out from under the covers. He leaned against the doorframe, arms crossed, looking infuriatingly well-rested. His dark hair was slightly tousled, his usual smirk firmly in place, and he was dressed in one of those tailored black jackets that made him look like he’d stepped out of a painting—one where the artist had taken *liberties* with the definition of “flattering.”

“You look like death,” he observed.

“And you look like you’ve never had a hangover in your life,” I muttered, pressing a hand to my temple. The movement sent a fresh wave of nausea through me. “Which is unfair.”

He chuckled, pushing off the doorframe and sauntering toward the bed. “I’ve had a few centuries to perfect my recovery.”

“Lucky you.” I squinted at him. “Did you *have* to let me drink that much?”

“You were the one who kept insisting on ‘one more glass.’” He sat on the edge of the bed, the mattress dipping under his weight. “I believe your exact words were, *‘I can handle it, Rhysand. I’m not some delicate human anymore.’*”

I winced. “I regret that.”

“Oh, I’m sure.” His fingers brushed against my wrist, sending a jolt of warmth up my arm. “But in your defense, you *did* handle it. Mostly.”

“Mostly,” I echoed, rubbing my face. The memory of stumbling into bed, Rhysand’s arm around my waist as he guided me, flickered through my mind. The way he’d pulled the blankets over me, his touch lingering just a second too long.

I lowered my hands. “Did we—”

“No,” he said quickly, too quickly, his smirk faltering for half a second. “Not that I wouldn’t have *liked* to, but you were in no state to make that kind of decision.”

I exhaled, relief and something dangerously close to disappointment twisting in my chest. “Good.”

“Good?” He raised a brow. “That’s all I get?”

“What do you want me to say?” I shot back, heat creeping into my cheeks. “That I’m *disappointed*?”

His grin turned wicked. “That would be a start.”

I threw a pillow at him. He caught it effortlessly, laughing, and tossed it aside. “As much as I enjoy watching you squirm, we *do* have things to discuss.”

The levity in his voice faded, and just like that, the weight of reality settled back onto my shoulders. I swallowed. “The revel. The things we learned.”

He nodded, his expression turning serious. “The Suriel’s warning. The threats from Hybern. The fact that we’re now officially *married* in the eyes of the courts.”

I glanced at the ring again. It felt heavier than it had last night. “Do you regret it?”

His fingers found mine, his thumb tracing the band. “No. Do *you*?”

I hesitated. Not because I wasn’t sure, but because the truth of it was still settling into my bones. “No,” I said finally. “But I’m… scared.”

He didn’t mock me for it. Didn’t tell me I was being ridiculous. He just squeezed my hand. “That’s fair.”

I exhaled, some of the tension uncoiling in my chest. “What do we do now?”

“We prepare,” he said simply. “We gather our allies. We fortify the borders. And we make sure Hybern knows that if they come for us, they won’t walk away.”

The steel in his voice sent a shiver down my spine. This was the Rhysand I’d first met—the ruthless High Lord of the Night Court, the man who had bargained with me in the woods all those months ago. The man who had looked at me like I was something precious, even when I’d been covered in blood and dirt.

I took a deep breath. “And the other courts?”

“They’ll come around,” he said, though his jaw tightened. “Eventually.”

I wanted to believe him. But I’d seen the way the other High Lords had looked at me last night—like I was an interloper, a human who had no business sitting at their table. Like I didn’t belong.

Rhysand must have seen the doubt in my eyes, because he cupped my face, his thumb brushing my cheekbone. “You *do* belong, Feyre,” he said softly. “With me. Here. In this court. And if anyone has a problem with that, they can take it up with me.”

The intensity in his gaze made my breath catch. For a moment, the weight of everything—the crown, the threats, the future—faded, and all I could see was him.

Then he ruined it by grinning. “Besides, if they *do* try anything, I’ll just remind them that you’re the one who painted that *masterpiece* of me in the library.”

I groaned, shoving him. “Don’t remind me.”

He laughed, catching my hand again, his fingers lacing with mine. “Too late.”

I shook my head, but I was smiling. Because despite everything—the hangover, the fear, the uncertainty—there was one thing I *wasn’t* afraid of.

Him.

And maybe, just maybe, that was enough.

**A Message from the Past**

The fire in the hearth had burned low, embers glowing like scattered stars against the dark, when the knock came.

I didn’t startle—Rhys had trained me better than that—but my fingers still tightened around the book in my lap. *A History of the Courts in the Middle Ages*, because apparently, even High Ladies needed bedtime reading that wouldn’t put them to sleep. The pages smelled of old parchment and ink, the weight of centuries pressing into my palms.

Another knock, softer this time. Almost hesitant.

I set the book aside, the spine cracking as I stood. The floorboards groaned under my fleece-lined boots, a familiar complaint. The heavy gray coat I’d swiped from Rhys’s closet—*borrowed*, I corrected myself—draped over the back of the chair, its red-and-gray scarf still tangled in the sleeves. Mor’s, if I remembered right. She’d never let me hear the end of it if she found out I’d taken it without asking.

I pulled the door open.

And froze.

The figure on the threshold was little more than a shadow, hood pulled low over their face, breath misting in the cold night air. But the scent—crisp pine and frost, with an undercurrent of something older, something wild—hit me like a physical blow.

“Hello, Feyre,” said Cassian, voice rough with disuse.

For a heartbeat, I just stared. Then I lunged.

He caught me easily, his laugh warm against my hair as I nearly knocked him over in my enthusiasm. “Miss me that much, did you?”

“You *bastard*,” I said, pulling back just enough to punch his arm. Hard. “Where the *hell* have you been?”

His grin was all sharp edges and wicked charm, but there was something strained around his eyes. “Busy.”

I crossed my arms. “Busy *ignoring* me, you mean.”

“Never.” He reached into his coat and pulled out a folded piece of parchment, sealed with wax the color of dried blood. “But I *did* bring you a present.”

I eyed the letter like it might bite me. “If this is another one of your ‘jokes,’ I swear—”

“It’s not from me.” His voice was suddenly serious, all traces of humor gone. “It’s from *her*.”

The world tilted.

I knew that seal. Had seen it only once before, pressed into the wax of a letter that had changed everything. A letter that had started a war.

“Nesta,” I breathed.

Cassian nodded, his jaw tight. “She didn’t want to send it through the usual channels. Said it had to be delivered by hand.”

I took the parchment, my fingers trembling. The wax was cold, brittle under my touch. “When did she—”

“A week ago.” His wings shifted restlessly behind him. “I was in the North. Took me a while to get here.”

A week. A *week*. And Nesta had sent this to *me*, not our sisters, not our parents. To *me*.

I broke the seal.

The letter was short, the handwriting neat and precise, so unlike the scrawling, angry script I’d seen from her before. But the words—

*Feyre,*

*If you’re reading this, then Cassian found you. Good. I need you to listen carefully.*

*There are things you don’t know. Things about the war. About the Suriel. About what really happened that night in the forest.*

*The truth is buried in the past. And if you dig it up, it might burn us all.*

*Be careful.*

*—N*

I read it twice. Then a third time.

Cassian was watching me, his expression unreadable. “You okay?”

I exhaled sharply. “No.”

“Yeah.” He rubbed the back of his neck. “That’s about what I figured.”

I looked up at him, the firelight casting shadows across his face. “You read it.”

“Had to make sure it wasn’t a death threat.”

I snorted. “And?”

“Still not sure.”

I folded the letter carefully, tucking it into my pocket. The parchment was thin, fragile. Like the truth it hinted at.

“What do you think she means?” I asked quietly.

Cassian’s wings rustled, a sound like wind through dead leaves. “I think,” he said slowly, “that your sister knows more about the war than she’s letting on. And I think she’s scared.”

Scared. Nesta.

The idea was almost laughable. Almost.

I looked past him, into the dark. Somewhere out there, the world was still turning. Still burning. And now, thanks to a single letter, I had no idea what to do next.

“Rhys is going to *lose it*,” I muttered.

Cassian grinned, sharp and sudden. “Oh, I’m counting on it.”

I punched him again. He laughed.

But my mind was already racing, already digging.

Because if Nesta was right—if the past held secrets that could change everything—then I had no choice but to find them.

No matter the cost.

**The Gathering Storm**

The fire in the hearth of the townhouse’s war room crackled like a living thing, casting flickering shadows across the faces of my inner circle. The scent of burning applewood mingled with the sharp tang of coffee—black, bitter, the way Rhys liked it—and the buttery richness of the pastries Cassian had somehow managed to swipe from the kitchens. My fingers tightened around my own steaming mug, the heat seeping through my gloves, grounding me.

No matter the cost.

The words echoed in my skull, a drumbeat beneath the murmur of conversation. Nesta’s warning still rang in my ears, her voice sharp as shattered glass. If the past held secrets, then we were running out of time to uncover them.

Rhys stood by the window, his back to the room, the winter light carving his silhouette into something stark and unyielding. His wings were tucked tight, the shadows at his feet writhing like restless serpents. He hadn’t said a word since we’d arrived, but I knew that silence. It was the quiet before the storm.

“Alright,” I said, setting my mug down with a deliberate *clink*. The room stilled. “Let’s hear it. What’s the damage?”

Cassian lounged in his chair, one booted foot propped on the table, a half-eaten pastry dangling from his fingers. “The damage,” he drawled, “is that the human lands are a powder keg, and someone’s about to light the fuse.”

Mor, seated beside him, rolled her eyes. “Eloquent as ever, Cassian.”

“You love it.”

“Like a hole in the head.”

I bit back a smile. “Focus.”

Azriel, ever the shadow, leaned forward, his Siphons glinting in the firelight. “The humans are restless. The treaty’s holding, but barely. Rumors are spreading—about us, about the Suriel, about what really happened at the Spring Court.” His voice was low, measured. “They’re scared. And scared people do stupid things.”

Rhys turned from the window at last, his violet eyes burning. “Like declaring war on the Night Court?”

A beat of silence.

“Not yet,” Azriel said carefully. “But the whispers are getting louder. The human queens are playing their games, but even they can’t ignore the unrest forever.”

I exhaled, rubbing my temples. “And the other courts?”

Cassian snorted. “Keir’s still sulking in the Hewn City, but he’s not stupid enough to make a move without backup. Beron’s too busy pretending he’s not terrified of his own shadow. And the Autumn Court—” He waved a hand. “Same old shit. Eris is still an asshole, but he’s not dumb enough to pick a fight with us. Not yet.”

“And the Spring Court?” I asked, already dreading the answer.

Mor’s expression darkened. “Tamlin’s locked himself away. No one’s seen him in weeks. The court’s in chaos—half of them think he’s gone mad, the other half think he’s plotting something.”

Rhys’s jaw tightened. “He’s not plotting. He’s drowning.”

I met his gaze, and something unspoken passed between us. Tamlin had been our enemy, our ally, our friend. Now? Now he was a ghost.

“So,” I said, forcing my voice steady, “we have humans on the verge of panic, a High Lord who’s one wrong move away from imploding, and a past that might come back to bite us in the ass. Did I miss anything?”

Cassian grinned, sharp and reckless. “Only that we’re all doomed.”

Mor kicked him under the table. “Shut up.”

I ignored them, turning to Rhys. “We need to send someone to the human lands. Someone who can smooth things over before they spiral.”

Rhys’s wings shifted, a ripple of darkness. “You’re thinking of going yourself.”

It wasn’t a question.

I lifted my chin. “I’m the High Lady. If anyone can calm them down, it’s me.”

“Absolutely not.” The words were a whip-crack, sharp enough to make Cassian wince.

I raised a brow. “Excuse me?”

Rhys’s shadows lashed, restless. “You’re not going into a nest of humans who think we’re monsters, Feyre. Not when they’re this close to breaking.”

“Then what do you suggest?” I snapped. “We sit here and wait for them to come for us?”

“No,” he said, voice dangerously calm. “We send someone else.”

“Who?” I demanded. “Mor? Cassian? Azriel? They’re not exactly *diplomatic*.”

Cassian grinned. “I’m plenty diplomatic.”

Mor elbowed him. “You once started a bar fight over a *look*.”

“It was a *threatening* look.”

I pinched the bridge of my nose. “This is getting us nowhere.”

Rhys’s gaze burned into mine. “We send Nesta.”

The room went still.

“What?” I said flatly.

“She’s human,” Rhys said, as if it were obvious. “She understands them. And she’s not afraid to tell them exactly where to shove it if they step out of line.”

Cassian barked a laugh. “Oh, this is going to be *fun*.”

Mor, however, looked skeptical. “Nesta? Really?”

I opened my mouth to argue, but—damn it—Rhys had a point. Nesta *was* human. She *did* understand their fears, their prejudices. And she had a way of cutting through bullshit that even Cassian couldn’t match.

But sending her into the human lands? After everything she’d been through?

“She’ll hate it,” I said at last.

Rhys’s mouth quirked. “Since when has that stopped us?”

I exhaled, long and slow. “Fine. But she’s not going alone. Azriel goes with her.”

Azriel inclined his head, already calculating. “It would be my honor.”

Cassian groaned. “Great. So we’re sending the human who hates us and the spymaster who scares the shit out of everyone. What could go wrong?”

Mor sighed. “Everything.”

I ignored them, turning back to Rhys. “And the past? The secrets Nesta mentioned?”

His expression darkened. “We dig. But carefully.”

I nodded. “Then we’re decided.”

The fire crackled, the only sound in the room for a long moment. Then Cassian stretched, cracking his knuckles. “So. Who’s telling Nesta?”

I groaned. “Oh, no. You’re not pinning this on me.”

“You’re the High Lady,” he said, grinning. “It’s your job.”

Rhys’s shadows coiled around me, warm and familiar. “I’ll help.”

I shot him a look. “Traitor.”

His grin was all teeth. “Always.”

And despite everything—the fear, the uncertainty, the weight of what was coming—I found myself smiling back.

Because if we were going to face the storm, at least we’d face it together.

**A Moment of Respite**

The firelight painted Rhysand’s face in flickering gold, softening the sharp angles of his features into something almost boyish. Almost. Because even now, with his wings tucked neatly against his back and his usual smirk replaced by something far gentler, there was no mistaking the predator beneath the charm.

“Always,” he’d said, and the word had settled into my bones like a promise.

I found myself smiling despite everything—the fear clawing at my ribs, the weight of what was coming, the way the air itself seemed to hum with tension. Because if we were going to face the storm, at least we’d face it together.

Rhys reached across the low table between us, his fingers brushing mine. “You’re thinking too loudly again.”

I huffed, nudging his boot with mine. “You’re one to talk. You’ve been brooding since we sat down.”

“Brooding?” He arched a brow, feigning offense. “I’ll have you know I was merely *strategizing*.”

“Mhm. And I’m the Suriel.”

His laugh was warm, rich, the kind that made my chest tighten. “Darling, if you were the Suriel, I’d have thrown myself off the nearest cliff by now.”

I threw a cushion at him. He caught it effortlessly, tossing it onto the couch behind him before leaning forward, elbows on his knees. The movement brought him closer, close enough that I could see the flecks of silver in his violet eyes, the way the firelight made them glow.

“You’re nervous,” he murmured.

I didn’t bother denying it. “Wouldn’t you be?”

“Of course.” His thumb traced idle circles over my knuckles. “But you’re forgetting something.”

“Oh?”

“You’re not alone in this.” His voice was low, steady, the kind of certainty that made my breath catch. “You’ve faced worse and come out the other side. And I’ll be right there with you.”

I swallowed hard. “What if I’m not enough?”

The words slipped out before I could stop them, raw and trembling. Rhys went very still, his expression softening into something achingly tender.

“Feyre.” He said my name like a prayer, like a vow. “You’ve already proven you’re more than enough. Time and time again.”

I wanted to believe him. Gods, I did. But the doubt coiled in my gut, a serpent waiting to strike.

Rhys must have seen it in my face, because he stood abruptly, pulling me up with him. His hands cradled my face, his thumbs brushing away the tension in my jaw. “Look at me,” he murmured.

I did.

“You are fierce. You are clever. You are *mine*.” His lips quirked, just a little. “And I am yours. And together, we are unstoppable.”

I let out a shaky breath, my hands finding their way to his waist. “You’re ridiculous.”

“And yet, you love me.”

I rolled my eyes, but the corner of my mouth betrayed me, lifting into a smile. “Unfortunately.”

His grin was wicked, triumphant. “Admit it. You’d be lost without me.”

“Don’t push your luck, High Lord.”

He laughed, low and rich, and then his arms were around me, pulling me flush against him. I buried my face in the crook of his neck, breathing in the scent of him—night and wind and something uniquely Rhysand. His fingers tangled in my braid, tilting my head back just enough to press a kiss to my forehead.

“No matter what happens,” he murmured against my skin, “we face it together. Always.”

I nodded, my throat tight. “Always.”

For a moment, the world narrowed to just this—the warmth of his body, the steady beat of his heart, the way his wings curled slightly around us like a shield. It was easy, in that moment, to forget the looming threat, the weight of responsibility, the fear gnawing at my edges.

But the moment was short-lived.

A sharp rap at the door shattered the quiet, and Rhys stiffened against me. Before either of us could move, the door swung open, revealing Cassian, his usual swagger replaced by something far more urgent.

“Sorry to interrupt,” he said, though his tone suggested he wasn’t sorry at all, “but we’ve got a problem.”

Rhys exhaled sharply, his arms loosening around me. “What is it?”

Cassian’s jaw tightened. “The human queens. They’ve made their move.”

The air left my lungs in a rush. Of course. Because peace was never meant to last.

Rhys’s fingers laced through mine, squeezing tight. “Then I suppose we’d best get back to work.”

I nodded, straightening my shoulders. The storm was coming. And we would face it—together.

But as I followed Rhys and Cassian out of the room, I couldn’t shake the feeling that the worst was yet to come.
