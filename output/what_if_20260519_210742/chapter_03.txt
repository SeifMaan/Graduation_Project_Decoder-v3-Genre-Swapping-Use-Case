CHAPTER 3
Shadows of the Past

**The Weight of Silence**

The Night Court welcomed us home with a breath of cold wind, sharp as a blade against my cheeks. The sky above Velaris stretched like a bruise—deep purples bleeding into the black of midnight, the stars winking as if they, too, were holding their breath. The city below glittered, a jewel in the dark, but its light felt distant, untouchable. The weight of what we had left behind pressed down on my shoulders, heavier than any armor.

Rhys’s hand found mine, his fingers warm despite the chill in the air. His thumb traced slow circles over my knuckles, a silent promise. *I’m here.* But even his touch couldn’t ease the knot in my chest.

We landed on the terrace of the townhouse, the stone cool beneath my boots. The familiar scent of salt and night-blooming jasmine should have been a comfort. Instead, it only sharpened the memory of smoke and blood, of the way the battlefield had reeked of iron and fear.

Rhys released my hand, his wings folding neatly against his back. His voice was low, careful. “They’ll be waiting inside.”

I nodded, but my throat was too tight to speak.

The moment we stepped through the doors, the air inside the townhouse thickened. Cassian stood by the fireplace, his arms crossed, his jaw set in that way that meant he was one wrong word away from snapping. Azriel lingered in the shadows near the window, his face unreadable, though the tension in his shoulders spoke volumes. Mor was curled in an armchair, her usual vibrancy dimmed, her fingers worrying the edge of a throw pillow. And Amren—Amren sat at the table, her silver eyes sharp as knives, her small frame radiating barely contained fury.

No one spoke.

The silence was a living thing, coiling around us, suffocating. I could feel their eyes on me, their questions, their judgments. *What did you do? What did you sacrifice? Why didn’t you tell us?*

Rhys moved to stand beside me, a steady presence at my back. “We need to talk,” he said, his voice cutting through the quiet.

Cassian exhaled sharply. “No shit.”

Mor flinched. Azriel’s shadows flickered, restless.

I swallowed hard. “I know you’re angry.”

“Angry?” Amren’s voice was a whip crack. “You think this is about anger?” She stood abruptly, the chair scraping against the floor. “You went into that battle with half a plan and no warning. You risked everything—*everything*—and for what?”

“For the people we’re supposed to protect,” I snapped, my own temper flaring. “For the future of Prythian. Or did you think we could just sit back and let Hybern burn it all to the ground?”

Amren’s eyes flashed. “I think you don’t trust us. Not really.”

The words hit like a physical blow. I opened my mouth, then closed it. Because she wasn’t wrong. Not entirely.

Rhys’s hand settled on the small of my back, a silent anchor. “Feyre did what she had to do,” he said quietly. “And she did it to keep us all alive.”

Cassian scoffed. “At what cost?”

The question hung in the air, heavy and unanswerable.

I looked at each of them—their faces tight with worry, with frustration, with fear. These were the people who had stood beside me, who had bled for me, who had *chosen* me. And yet, I had kept them in the dark. I had made decisions that would shape their lives without giving them a say.

The weight of it threatened to crush me.

Rhys must have sensed the shift in me, because his hand pressed firmer against my back. “Give us a moment,” he said to the others.

No one argued. One by one, they filed out, leaving us alone in the quiet.

The door clicked shut behind them. The silence returned, thicker now, laden with everything left unsaid.

I turned to Rhys, my vision blurring. “What if they’re right?” The words tasted like ash. “What if I’m not—what if I’m not enough?”

Rhys cupped my face in his hands, his thumbs brushing away the tears I hadn’t even realized were falling. His eyes, those endless depths of violet and starlight, held mine with a steadiness that made my chest ache. “You are more than enough,” he murmured. “You always have been.”

I wanted to believe him. Gods, I wanted to.

But the doubt coiled inside me, cold and relentless. “I didn’t tell them,” I whispered. “I didn’t tell *you*. Not everything.”

Rhys’s expression didn’t change, but I saw the flicker of pain in his eyes. “I know.”

“I was afraid,” I admitted, my voice breaking. “Afraid that if I told you what I was planning, you’d try to stop me. Afraid that if I told them what I’d done, they’d look at me the way they’re looking at me now.”

Rhys pulled me against him, his arms wrapping around me like a shield. “You don’t have to carry this alone,” he said, his breath warm against my hair. “Not anymore.”

I buried my face in his chest, inhaling the scent of him—night and wind and something wild and untamed. For the first time since the battle, I let myself breathe.

But the weight of silence remained.

And I knew, deep down, that some things couldn’t be unspoken forever.

---

**Whispers in the Dark**

The fire in the hearth had burned low, its embers pulsing like a slow, dying heartbeat. I sat curled in the armchair, fingers tracing the edge of the book in my lap—pages unread, words blurring into nothing. The weight of silence pressed down on me, thick and suffocating, the kind that settled into bones and refused to be shaken.

I knew it couldn’t last.

The door creaked open, and Morrigan slipped inside like a shadow given form. Her dark hair was loose, spilling over her shoulders in waves that seemed to drink in the dim light. She didn’t speak at first, just leaned against the doorframe, arms crossed, her golden eyes sharp as blades.

“You’ve been avoiding me,” she said, voice low.

I didn’t look up. “I’ve been reading.”

A lie. We both knew it.

Mor’s laugh was a dry, humorless thing. “Reading. Right.” She pushed off the doorframe and crossed the room in three strides, plucking the book from my hands before I could protest. The spine cracked as she flipped it shut, the sound too loud in the quiet. “You haven’t turned a single page in an hour.”

I exhaled through my nose, rubbing my temples. “What do you want, Mor?”

“For you to stop pretending you’re fine.” She tossed the book onto the side table, where it landed with a thud. “You think I don’t notice the way you flinch when someone touches you? The way you jump at every shadow? You’re not fine, Feyre. You haven’t been fine since—”

“Don’t.” The word came out sharper than I intended. I stood abruptly, the chair scraping against the floor. “Just… don’t.”

Mor didn’t back down. She never did. She stepped closer, close enough that I could see the flecks of amber in her eyes, the way her pupils dilated in the firelight. “I’m not your enemy,” she said softly. “But I won’t let you drown in this.”

“I’m not drowning.”

“Then why do you look like you’re one breath away from shattering?”

I turned away, my chest tight. The fire’s warmth licked at my skin, but it did nothing to thaw the cold knot in my stomach. “I don’t know what you want me to say.”

“The truth,” Mor said, voice steady. “For once.”

The truth.

The truth was that I woke up some nights gasping, my hands clawing at the sheets, convinced I was still back in that cell, still chained to the wall with Hybern’s knife at my throat. The truth was that I flinched when Rhysand reached for me, not because I didn’t want him to touch me, but because I was terrified of what I might see in his eyes when he did. The truth was that I didn’t know how to be *here*—not like this. Not when the war was over, and the world was supposed to be different, but I wasn’t.

I swallowed hard. “I don’t know how to do this.”

Mor’s expression softened. “Do what?”

“Live.” The word tasted like ash. “I don’t know how to live like this.”

She was quiet for a long moment. Then, slowly, she reached out and took my hand. Her fingers were warm, calloused. “You don’t have to have it all figured out,” she said. “But you can’t keep pushing everyone away.”

I wanted to argue. I wanted to tell her she didn’t understand, that no one did. But the look in her eyes—steady, unyielding—made the words die in my throat.

Before I could respond, the door swung open again.

Azriel stood in the doorway, his wings half-furled, shadows clinging to him like a second skin. His face was grim, his jaw tight. “We have a problem.”

Mor dropped my hand, her posture shifting instantly, all softness gone. “What is it?”

Azriel’s gaze flicked to me, then back to Mor. “Human lands. There’s movement—unrest. Armies gathering near the borders.”

My stomach twisted. “Hybern?”

“No,” Azriel said. “Humans. And not the kind we’ve dealt with before.”

Mor’s brows knit together. “What does that mean?”

“It means,” Azriel said, his voice low, “that they’re not just preparing for war. They’re preparing for *us*.”

The fire crackled, the sound suddenly too loud, too sharp. I looked between them, my pulse hammering in my throat. The peace we’d fought for—it had never been more than a fragile thing. And now, it was unraveling.

Mor exhaled sharply. “Rhys needs to hear this.”

Azriel nodded, but his eyes stayed on me. “He’s already on his way.”

I didn’t move. Didn’t breathe. The weight of it all pressed down on me, heavier than before. The war was over. But the shadows remained.

And they were whispering.

**The Unseen Enemy**

The war was over.

But the shadows remained.

And they were whispering.

The words slithered through my mind as I stepped into the dimly lit study, the scent of aged parchment and smoldering embers thick in the air. The fire in the hearth crackled low, casting flickering gold across the faces of the inner circle—each one tense, each one waiting.

Azriel stood near the window, his wings half-furled, the shadows clinging to him like a second skin. His report lay spread across the table, the ink still glistening where he had scrawled the final lines. The words were stark, unyielding: *Movement in the north. Not human. Not Fae. Something else.*

Cassian leaned against the edge of the desk, arms crossed, his jaw set in that familiar stubborn line. "We hit them now," he said, voice rough with barely restrained frustration. "Before they have a chance to dig in."

Amren’s fingers drummed against the armrest of her chair, the sound sharp as a blade against stone. "And risk another war?" Her silver eyes gleamed with cold amusement. "You’re as reckless as you were a century ago, boy."

Cassian’s wings twitched. "And you’re as cautious as a rabbit in a fox’s den."

Rhysand said nothing.

He stood apart from the rest of us, his back to the room, his gaze fixed on the city beyond the window. The Night Court sprawled beneath us, a sea of twinkling lights, but his silence was a void in the room, swallowing the tension whole.

I studied him—the way his fingers curled slightly at his sides, the way his shoulders held a stiffness that wasn’t there before. He knew something. Something he wasn’t saying.

My chest tightened.

"Rhys," I said softly.

He didn’t turn. Didn’t even flinch.

Amren’s lips curled. "He’s been like that since Azriel walked in."

Cassian exhaled sharply. "Great. Just what we need."

I ignored them, stepping closer to Rhys. The scent of him—night-blooming jasmine and something darker, something like storm clouds—wrapped around me, familiar and unsettling. "What aren’t you telling us?"

For a heartbeat, I thought he wouldn’t answer. Then, slowly, he turned. His face was a mask, but his eyes—his eyes were a storm of unspoken words.

Before he could speak, the door burst open.

A messenger stumbled in, her breath ragged, her clothes splattered with mud. The scent of damp earth and fear clung to her. "My lord," she gasped, bowing low. "The Spring Court—"

The words died in her throat as she saw me.

I knew that look. The way her gaze flickered over me, recognition sharpening into something like pity. My stomach twisted.

"Feyre," she whispered.

Cassian straightened, his wings flaring slightly. "What’s happened?"

The messenger swallowed hard. "The Spring Court is under attack. Not by humans. Not by Fae." Her voice dropped to a whisper. "By shadows."

The room went still.

Rhysand’s expression didn’t change. But his fingers curled into fists at his sides.

And I—

I felt the weight of my past pressing down on me, the ghosts of that court rising like specters in the corners of my mind. Tamlin. The estate. The life I had left behind.

The messenger’s next words were a blade to the chest.

"They’re saying it’s her fault."

Silence.

Then Cassian’s growl cut through the room. "The hell it is."

But Rhysand’s gaze was on me, dark and unreadable.

And I knew, with a sinking certainty, that the shadows weren’t just whispering anymore.

They were coming for me.

---

**A Choice Revisited**

The weight of Rhysand’s gaze pressed against my skin like a physical touch, heavy and unyielding. The air in the room had thickened, charged with something electric and dangerous. I could feel the shadows curling at the edges of my vision, not as threats, but as extensions of him—of *us*—stretching toward me with silent insistence.

I swallowed hard, my fingers tightening around the letter in my hand. The parchment was brittle, the ink smudged in places where my grip had trembled. The message from the Spring Court was a blade wrapped in silk, its edges sharp enough to draw blood if I wasn’t careful.

*She is not forgotten.*

The words echoed in my skull, a taunt, a reminder. A choice I had made long ago, one that had shaped everything since.

Rhysand didn’t move from where he stood by the window, his silhouette framed against the dim glow of the city below. The Night Court stretched out beneath us, a kingdom of stars and secrets, but even its beauty couldn’t distract me from the storm brewing inside me.

“You’re thinking too loudly,” he murmured, his voice a low rumble that vibrated through the space between us. “It’s giving me a headache.”

I shot him a glare, but the effect was ruined by the way my throat tightened. “This isn’t a joke.”

“I never said it was.” He turned then, his wings flaring slightly before settling against his back. The movement was deliberate, a reminder of the power he carried, the control. “But you’re spiraling, and I’d rather not watch you drown in it.”

I exhaled sharply, the letter crumpling further in my grip. “What if I made the wrong choice?”

The words hung between us, raw and ugly. Rhysand’s expression didn’t change, but his eyes—those endless, star-strewn eyes—darkened. “You don’t believe that.”

“Don’t I?” I stepped forward, my boots clicking against the obsidian floor. “What if I was selfish? What if I—”

“Feyre.” His voice was a whip-crack, sharp enough to silence me. “You saved lives. You saved *her* life. That’s not selfishness. That’s courage.”

I wanted to believe him. Gods, I wanted to. But the doubt coiled in my chest, a serpent waiting to strike. “And if it wasn’t enough?”

Rhysand closed the distance between us in two strides, his hands gripping my shoulders. His touch was warm, grounding, but the intensity in his gaze burned. “Enough for who? For *you*?”

I flinched. “I don’t—”

“You do.” His thumb brushed the edge of my jaw, tilting my face up to meet his. “You’ve spent years running from this, from the guilt, from the fear. But you can’t outrun it forever.”

The truth of his words hit me like a physical blow. I *had* been running. From the memories, from the choices, from the part of myself that still ached with the weight of what I’d done—and what I’d left undone.

The letter in my hand suddenly felt like a brand. I looked down at it, at the elegant script that had once been familiar, now twisted into something foreign and dangerous.

“What if they’re right?” I whispered. “What if I *did* fail her?”

Rhysand’s fingers tightened, not painfully, but insistently. “You didn’t.”

“You don’t know that.”

“I know *you*.” His voice was fierce, unyielding. “I know the woman who stood in the snow and bled for strangers. I know the woman who faced Amarantha and didn’t break. And I know the woman who looks at me like I’m the only thing keeping her from shattering.” His thumb traced the line of my cheekbone, his touch achingly gentle. “You didn’t fail her. You *chose*. And that’s not something to regret.”

The tears came then, hot and sudden, spilling over before I could stop them. Rhysand didn’t look away. He never did. He just pulled me against him, his arms wrapping around me like a shield.

I buried my face in his chest, breathing in the scent of him—night and wind and something wild. “I’m scared,” I admitted, my voice muffled against his shirt.

“I know.” His hand cradled the back of my head, his fingers tangling in my hair. “But you don’t have to face it alone.”

I pulled back just enough to meet his gaze. The shadows around us pulsed, alive and restless, but there was no fear in them now. Only him. Only *us*.

The letter felt heavier than ever in my hand. I looked down at it, at the words that had once been a lifeline, now a chain. And then, with a slow exhale, I made my choice.

I let it fall.

The parchment fluttered to the floor, forgotten. Rhysand’s eyes followed it, then lifted to mine, questioning.

I squared my shoulders. “We’re not hiding from this.”

A slow, dangerous smile curved his lips. “No?”

“No.” I stepped back, my pulse steadying. “If they want a fight, they’ll get one. But not on their terms. On *ours*.”

Rhysand’s smile widened, sharp and triumphant. “There she is.”

And just like that, the storm inside me stilled. The shadows no longer whispered—they roared.

I was done running.
