Chapter 1

**Chapter 1: Scene 1**

The air in the alley was thick with the scent of damp brick and something sharper—ozone, maybe, or the metallic tang of old blood. Elias crouched in the shadows, his breath shallow, fingers curled around the hilt of the knife at his belt. The cobblestones beneath him were slick with rain that had fallen earlier, the puddles reflecting the flicker of gas lamps from the street beyond.

He wasn’t supposed to be here.

But then, he wasn’t supposed to be *anywhere* anymore.

A rat skittered past his boot, its claws clicking against the stone. He didn’t flinch. He was used to things that scuttled in the dark. What made his pulse spike was the sound of footsteps—slow, deliberate—echoing from the mouth of the alley.

Elias pressed himself against the wall, his shoulder blades digging into the rough mortar. The footsteps stopped. A match flared, casting a brief, golden glow over a face he knew too well.

*Veyra.*

She leaned against the alley’s edge, the match cupped in her palm, the flame licking at her fingertips before she shook it out. The scent of sulfur joined the other smells of the night. “You’re late,” she said, her voice low, amused.

Elias exhaled through his nose. “I didn’t agree to meet you.”

“No?” Veyra pushed off the wall, stepping into the alley proper. Her boots made no sound on the wet stone. “Then what are you doing here, *little ghost*?”

The nickname grated. He wasn’t a ghost. Not yet. But he didn’t correct her. Instead, he straightened, letting his hand fall away from the knife. “I’m leaving the city.”

Veyra laughed, a sound like breaking glass. “Oh, Elias. You’ve been *leaving* the city for three years now.” She tilted her head, her dark eyes glinting in the dim light. “Funny how you always end up back in the same alleys.”

He clenched his jaw. She wasn’t wrong. But this time was different. This time, he had a ship waiting at the docks, a name scratched onto a manifest, a life that didn’t involve looking over his shoulder every five seconds.

“What do you want, Veyra?”

She took another step closer, close enough that he could see the scar bisecting her eyebrow, the way her lips curled into something that wasn’t quite a smile. “I want you to work for me.”

Elias barked out a laugh. “No.”

“No?” She arched a brow. “Just like that?”

“Just like that.”

Veyra sighed, as if he were a child refusing to eat his vegetables. “You’re making a mistake.”

“Then it’s mine to make.”

For a long moment, she just stared at him. Then, with a suddenness that made him tense, she reached into her coat and pulled out a folded slip of paper. She held it out between two fingers. “Take it.”

Elias didn’t move. “What is it?”

“A name. An address.” She wiggled it slightly. “A job.”

“I don’t want it.”

“You don’t even know what it is.”

“I don’t care.”

Veyra’s expression darkened. “You *should* care.” She stepped forward, pressing the paper into his palm. Her fingers were cold. “Because if you don’t take this job, Elias, you’re going to find out exactly how little your life is worth.”

He looked down at the paper, his thumb brushing over the ink. A name. *Lirien Veyne.* An address in the merchant quarter. And beneath it, a single word: *Alive.*

His stomach twisted.

Veyra stepped back, her boots clicking against the stone. “You have until dawn. After that, the offer expires.” She turned, her coat flaring out behind her like wings. “And so, I suspect, will your luck.”

Elias didn’t watch her leave. He stared at the paper, the ink smudged in places, as if written in haste. The name meant nothing to him. But the word beneath it—*alive*—meant everything.

Because he knew what happened to people who crossed Veyra.

And he knew what happened to people who *didn’t.*

The rat from before darted past his feet again, this time pausing to sniff at the hem of his coat. Elias exhaled, crumpling the paper in his fist.

Dawn was in four hours.

He had a choice to make.
