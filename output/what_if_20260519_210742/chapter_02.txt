Chapter 2

**Chapter 2 | Scene 1**

The paper in Elias’s fist was damp with sweat, its edges biting into his palm like the teeth of a small, furious animal. He exhaled, the breath curling into the cold air of the stable loft, where the scent of hay and leather clung to everything. Below, the horses shifted in their stalls, their hooves scraping against packed earth, their warm bodies radiating the last of the day’s heat. The barn was quiet except for the occasional rustle of straw, the whisper of a tail swishing against wood.

Four hours until dawn.

Elias unfolded his fingers one by one, smoothing the crumpled paper against his thigh. The ink had bled in places where his grip had been too tight, but the words were still legible. *Meet at the old mill. Midnight. Come alone.* No signature, no seal—just the stark, demanding script of a man who didn’t need to sign his name to be recognized.

Or a trap.

He crumpled it again, tighter this time, as if he could squeeze the decision out of it. The mill was half a day’s ride from Black Hollow, deep in the woods where the trees grew so thick they blotted out the sky. A good place to disappear. A better place to die.

A floorboard creaked behind him.

Elias didn’t turn. He didn’t need to. The scent of pine and gunpowder preceded her, sharp and familiar. “You’re still here,” he said.

Seraphine dropped onto the hay bale beside him, the movement effortless, like a cat settling into a sunbeam. She was all lean muscle and restless energy, her dark hair tied back in a knot that was already coming undone. A smudge of soot streaked her cheekbone, and her fingers—long, capable—tapped against the hilt of the knife sheathed at her hip.

“Where else would I be?” she asked, voice dry. “You look like a man who’s about to do something stupid.”

Elias barked a laugh, short and humorless. “That’s rich, coming from you.”

She grinned, quick and bright, but it didn’t reach her eyes. “I prefer the term *reckless*. There’s a difference.” Her gaze flicked to the paper in his hand. “That the note?”

“Mm.”

“And?”

“And what?”

Seraphine rolled her eyes. “And are you going?”

The question hung between them, heavy as the coming storm. Elias flexed his fingers, watching the way the lamplight played over the scars on his knuckles—old wounds, most of them, but some still tender. “I don’t know.”

“Bullshit.” She leaned forward, elbows on her knees. “You’ve already decided. You’re just trying to talk yourself out of it.”

He wanted to deny it. Wanted to lie and say he was weighing his options, considering the consequences. But Seraphine had always seen through him, even when he was lying to himself. “It’s a trap,” he said instead.

“Probably.”

“He’ll have men there. Waiting.”

“Undoubtedly.”

Elias shot her a look. “You’re not helping.”

She shrugged. “I’m not here to help. I’m here to make sure you don’t get yourself killed.” Her fingers stilled on the knife hilt. “You go in there alone, you’re dead. You know that.”

“I know.”

“So don’t go alone.”

The words settled over him like a noose. Elias closed his eyes. He could still hear the echo of his father’s voice, rough with age and whiskey: *A man’s got to stand on his own two feet, boy. No one else is gonna do it for him.* But his father had been wrong about a lot of things. About honor. About loyalty. About the kind of man Elias was supposed to be.

“I can’t ask you to do this,” he said quietly.

Seraphine scoffed. “You’re not asking. I’m telling.” She stood, brushing hay from her trousers. “We leave in an hour. That gives you just enough time to pack and say whatever sentimental goodbyes you need to make.”

Elias stared at her. “You’re serious.”

“Dead serious.” She smirked. “Pun intended.”

He should have argued. Should have insisted she stay behind, safe. But the truth was, he didn’t want to go alone. And if he was being honest with himself, he never had.

“Fine,” he said, pushing to his feet. The hay crackled under his boots. “But if we die, I’m haunting you first.”

Seraphine’s laughter followed him down the ladder, bright and sharp as a blade. “Deal.”

---

The night air was bitter when they stepped outside, the kind of cold that seeped into your bones and stayed there. Elias pulled his coat tighter, the wool rough against his skin. The stable yard was empty, the other hands long since retired to the bunkhouse. Only the wind moved, rattling the loose shutters on the blacksmith’s shed, sending dead leaves skittering across the packed earth.

Seraphine adjusted the saddlebags on her horse, her breath misting in the air. “You sure about this?”

Elias checked the cinch on his own saddle, his fingers numb with cold. “No.”

“Good. Means you’re not completely stupid.” She swung up onto her mare, the animal shifting beneath her with barely restrained energy. “Let’s move. We’ve got a long ride ahead.”

Elias mounted, the leather creaking under his weight. The note was a lead weight in his pocket, burning through the fabric. He didn’t know what waited for them at the mill. Didn’t know if he’d walk away from this.

But he knew one thing for certain.

He wasn’t going to face it alone.
